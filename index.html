Client A · Hospitality · Houston, TX
$600K+
Gross revenue increase · one unit · $2M+ per unit · two locations

A 42-year-old institution that had never advertised.
I changed that. The numbers followed.

Phase 1 · Foundation & Compliance

Safety, plant, staff — in that order.

Walked in to find a fire suppression system two years out of date. Fixed before anything else. Then ventilation, electrical — equipment running on extension cords — and a cooler that needed relining. Nothing else matters if the foundation isn't safe and compliant. Staff evaluated against actual capability, not current title. A food runner who was a trained cook elsewhere was moved, given a raise to compensate for lost tip share, and placed correctly. A line cook creating friction was replaced with a known quantity. Ticket times dropped fifty percent.

Phase 2 · Menu & Margin

Waste streams converted to revenue.

Recosted the entire menu at actual 2007–2008 market prices. Then looked at what was being thrown away. Tuna scraps became $15-a-plate seared tuna logs with strawberry, avocado, and balsamic drizzle. Beef trim became carpaccio. Salmon shavings became lemon pepper skewers. Cook-off fish became bite-sized fish cakes with sour cream sauce. Sweet potato and polenta — made fresh daily — combined at end of day, chilled overnight, cut into cakes, dusted in flour, deep fried. Zero additional food cost. New menu item. Leftover roasted chicken combined with the polenta cakes and a housemade chipotle ranch became another composed dish.

The owner wanted a chocolate lava cake. I held my ground. Chocolate lava cakes are popular — and everyone can get one everywhere. What you really want is something so good people can't get it anywhere else. Introduced my recipe for white chocolate blueberry bread pudding — croissant base, dried blueberry pieces, white chocolate drizzle. When In Town magazine featured the restaurant, that was one of the items they photographed. Differentiation over trend. Every time.

Phase 3 · Revenue Ecosystem

Happy hour as customer acquisition. Vendor partnerships as revenue events.

Reinvented happy hour from institutional mediocrity into an experience that drove traffic and word of mouth. The argument with the owner: the price of a drink is the price of a drink. The food is what draws the people. Won the argument. Built a business card collection system and email marketing — in 2007. Partnered with the liquor vendor to turn routine rep visits into tasting events where customers could buy full bottles on the spot. Expanded catering. Added city holiday parties. Built the flywheel.

Phase 4 · Culture & Operations

Engagement, punctuality, and sales training built into the daily routine.

I created a space for the cooks to come into my office after the lunch rush — sit down, eat together, decompress. Do you have something you'd like to make for next week's special? Specials rotated every Tuesday. The cooks drove the ideas. Every Tuesday morning, fifteen minutes before service, the whole kitchen made the weekly special and everyone tasted it — the bartender sommelier recommended a wine pairing. Staff went onto the floor knowing exactly what they were selling. That's not a tasting. That's a sales meeting.

Every Friday morning I cooked breakfast for the staff — pancakes, sausage, bacon, sometimes barbacoa and breakfast tacos. If you wanted Friday breakfast, you had to be there early. Punctuality solved through culture, not discipline. They loved the pancakes. Any time a menu item changed, every server tasted it. New servers tasted the entire menu before their first shift. You can't sell what you haven't experienced.

Client B · Private Estate · 18 Years
$324K+
Annual savings · vendor restructure + facilities & infrastructure rebuild

Full estate management — financial, operational, and infrastructure — for eighteen years.

Engagement · Financial & Vendor Audit

Read the system. Find where value is being lost.

Took over financial management for a private household. Audited existing vendor relationships — every service, every contract, every recurring cost. Evaluated service quality against actual cost. Identified where equivalent or better service was available at lower cost. Restructured the vendor portfolio. $18,000+ saved annually — $324,000+ over the engagement — without sacrificing service quality.

Facilities & Property Management

Responsible for all maintenance of the property.

Full responsibility for property maintenance including landscaping and all physical plant. The house was built in 1974 — due to its age, it was time to reclad and paint the exterior. Had the house completely reclad in HardiBoard. The original electrical was designed for 1974 load requirements — modern living demands far more outlets and capacity. Had the electrical system upgraded throughout to compensate. Found an exterior panel that was out of code and had it replaced. Stewardship decisions, not crisis response.

Infrastructure Engineering

Research, specify, install, optimize.

The upstairs converted attic space was running hot and expensive. Researched and specified a full HVAC system upgrade. Had the space properly insulated to reduce the operating load before the new system went in. To prevent debris from collecting in the new unit outside, I designed a custom liftable cover using solar screen material. Full airflow. No debris ingestion. Shaded from direct heat.

Lower operating temperature. Lower energy cost. Longer equipment life. That's not a maintenance decision. That's an engineering decision.
