The Name

My father spent his career correcting the assumption. He founded CyberPhase International in the early 1980s and watched people read the name and immediately say "Oh, you're in IT." He wasn't. He was a planning and scheduling specialist who happened to use computers — at a time when that was still remarkable.

The discipline came first. The technology was the tool. He built something rooted in ancient philosophy — the art of the helmsman, the navigator, the one who holds the course through complexity — and the world kept filing it under a job category that didn't exist yet when he started.

The helm in the logo isn't decoration. It's the point. It's been there since 1983.

George Douglas Wise Cosby
Federal Revenuer · Texas Ranger · Virginia to Texas, 1920s

Worked for the predecessor to the ATF, finding illegal stills by watching for drunk animals when others looked for smoke. So effective that moonshiners pooled resources to put a price on his head. The government moved him to Texas. He signed up with the Rangers and kept doing what he had always done. He taught my father how to read the world.

George Douglas Cosby
Navy Radar Engineer · Government Service · Planning & Scheduling Pioneer · Founder, CyberPhase International

He helped develop radar as we know it — technology built to find signal through noise, through the static, through the storm. His career spanned Navy radar engineering, government service during one of the most complex and dangerous periods in American history, and the full evolution of planning and scheduling — from index cards and string down a hallway to computerized systems. He founded CyberPhase International to help organizations navigate from the analog world into the digital one. Author. Artist. The man who named the firm, passed the discipline forward, and taught his daughter everything he knew about reading systems, finding patterns, and navigating chaos toward outcomes. The problems have evolved. The discipline hasn't.

Janice Marie Cosby
Data Analyst · Bernard Johnson Engineering, 1977 · Professor, San Jacinto College

The sole female data analyst at Bernard Johnson Engineering in Houston in 1977. Built automated documentation programs for the City of Houston's Emergency Broadcast System when computers were just arriving in the workforce. Later a professor at San Jacinto College, teaching the next generation of programmers. She broke ground quietly, without making it a statement.
